How to Use the Gravel Calculator

Enter the Area You Want to Cover

Add each area as a rectangle, circle or triangle. Use metres for lengths, widths, bases, heights and diameters. The calculator applies one shared depth and one selected material density to all areas in that calculation. For an irregular space, split it into simple non-overlapping shapes.

Choose the Required Gravel Depth

Next, enter how deep the gravel layer needs to be. Depth is usually measured in millimetres, and the calculator converts it to metres for you. As a guide, decorative areas and paths need around 25 to 50mm, while driveways often need 75 to 100mm or more. See the depth section below for more detail.

Calculate the Total Gravel Volume and Quantity

As you enter or change valid measurements, the result updates automatically. The tool multiplies length, width and depth to give the volume in cubic metres. It also shows an estimated weight in tonnes, because most suppliers sell gravel by weight or by the cubic metre.

Estimate How Much Gravel to Order

The calculated figure is the amount needed to fill the space exactly. In practice, gravel settles and compacts, and some is lost during spreading. The calculator combines compaction and waste in one allowance field. It starts at 15%, and you can select no allowance, 5%, 10%, 15% or enter a custom percentage. Choose the allowance that suits the material and site; do not add the same compaction or waste percentage again outside the calculator unless you have a separate reason. Round up to the nearest practical order size, such as the next half cubic metre or the next full tonne.

How Much Gravel Do You Need for Your Project?

The right quantity depends on the size of the area and the job the gravel has to do. Here are the most common projects and what to expect.

Gravel Requirements for Driveways and Parking Areas

Driveways carry vehicle loads, so they need a deeper layer than most other projects. A typical top layer is 75 to 100mm, often laid over a compacted base of crushed rock or road base. For example, a driveway that is 20 metres long and 3 metres wide at 100mm deep needs 20 × 3 × 0.1 = 6m³ of gravel. Parking bays and turning areas follow the same method: measure the area, choose the depth and calculate.

Gravel Quantity for Garden Paths and Walkways

Paths only carry foot traffic, so a thinner layer of 25 to 50mm is usually enough. A path that is 10 metres long and 1 metre wide at 50mm deep needs 10 × 1 × 0.05 = 0.5m³. If you are using edging, measure the width between the edges rather than the overall width of the path.

Gravel Requirements for Landscaping and Decorative Areas

For garden beds, dry creek beds, around pots and other decorative areas, a depth of 25 to 50mm gives good coverage and a tidy finish. A bed that is 3 metres by 2 metres at 50mm deep needs 3 × 2 × 0.05 = 0.3m³. Placing weed control fabric underneath helps keep the gravel clean and the weeds down.

Calculating Gravel for Multiple or Irregularly Shaped Areas

Most real projects are not perfect rectangles. Break the space into simple shapes, calculate each one separately and add the volumes together.

  • Rectangles: length × width
  • Triangles: ½ × base × height
  • Circles: π × radius × radius (about 3.14 × r²)

For example, a circular feature area with a radius of 1.5 metres has an area of about 7.07m². At 50mm deep, that needs roughly 0.35m³ of gravel. If you have several separate areas, run the calculator for each and add the totals before ordering.

How Is Gravel Quantity Calculated?

Gravel Volume Formula: Area × Depth

The formula is simple:

Volume (m³) = Length (m) × Width (m) × Depth (m)

All three measurements must be in metres. The answer comes out in cubic metres, which is the unit most Australian suppliers use for bulk gravel.

Converting Measurements into Cubic Metres

Depth is the measurement most people get wrong, because it is normally quoted in millimetres or centimetres. To convert:

  • Millimetres to metres: divide by 1,000 (100mm = 0.1m)
  • Centimetres to metres: divide by 100 (10cm = 0.1m)

Some common depths in metres: 25mm = 0.025m, 50mm = 0.05m, 75mm = 0.075m, 100mm = 0.1m and 150mm = 0.15m.

Understanding Gravel Volume, Density and Weight

Gravel is sold by volume (cubic metres) or by weight (tonnes), so it helps to know how the two relate. Loose gravel typically weighs between 1.4 and 1.7 tonnes per cubic metre, depending on the stone type, size and moisture. Crushed rock and road base can be heavier, especially once compacted. A good rule of thumb for general garden gravel is about 1.5 tonnes per cubic metre. Always confirm the exact figure with your supplier, as it varies between products.

How to Calculate Gravel Requirements Using a Worked Example

Let’s say you are laying pebbles or 20mm gravel over an area that is 5 metres long and 4 metres wide, at a depth of 100mm. Select that material preset (1.5 tonnes per m³) and keep the calculator’s starting 15% combined compaction-and-waste allowance.

  • Convert the depth: 100mm = 0.1m
  • Calculate the volume before allowance: 5 × 4 × 0.1 = 2m³
  • Add the 15% allowance: 2 × 1.15 = 2.3m³
  • Estimate the weight: 2.3m³ × 1.5 tonnes per m³ = 3.45 tonnes

So the calculator estimates about 2.3m³ or 3.45 tonnes. If you choose another gravel type, use its selected density; the weight will change.

What Depth of Gravel Should You Use?

Recommended Gravel Depth for Driveways

For driveways, a finished gravel layer of 75 to 100mm is a common choice. If the ground is soft or the driveway takes heavy vehicles, a base layer of 100 to 150mm of compacted crushed rock beneath the gravel gives better stability. Thin layers tend to rut and show the soil underneath, so avoid going too shallow.

Gravel Depth for Garden Paths and Landscaping

Garden paths and decorative areas do not need as much depth. A layer of 25 to 50mm is usually enough to cover the ground and look full. Go closer to 50mm if you want better weed suppression or if the gravel will be walked on regularly.

What Factors Affect the Amount of Gravel You Need?

Gravel Type, Size and Density

Different gravels have different weights and coverage. Fine pea gravel packs more tightly than large river pebbles, and crushed rock is denser than rounded stone. Larger stones also need a greater depth to cover the ground properly, as a thin layer of big stones leaves gaps. Check the product size and recommended depth before you finalise the quantity.

Ground Conditions and Existing Surface Levels

Soft, sandy or clay soils can swallow some of the gravel as it settles. If the ground is uneven, measure the average depth rather than the shallowest point. Low spots will take more gravel, and high spots will take less. If you are laying gravel over an existing surface, deduct the thickness of any material that stays in place.

Compaction and Material Settlement

Gravel compacts once it is spread, rolled or driven over. The layer you lay is often thicker than the final result, and the surface will drop slightly over the first few weeks. Allowing for this at the ordering stage means you will not need to top up straight away.

Allowing Extra Gravel for Uneven Surfaces and Wastage

A sensible allowance is 5 to 10% for flat, well prepared areas and 10 to 15% for uneven ground or large driveways. Some gravel is also lost during delivery, spreading and shovelling. It is almost always cheaper to order a little extra than to arrange a second small delivery.

Buying Gravel: Bulk Delivery vs Bags

How Many Bags of Gravel Do You Need?

Bags are convenient for small jobs. The calculator first estimates the total material weight using the selected density, then divides that weight by the chosen bag weight. It offers 20 kg, 25 kg or a custom bag size. To do a manual check, divide the total weight in kilograms by the bag weight. For example, 1m³ of gravel at about 1.5 tonnes is roughly 1,500kg. Using 20kg bags, that is around 75 bags. Using 25kg bags, it is around 60 bags. Check the bag size and weight on the product label, as these vary between brands.

How Much Does a Cubic Metre of Gravel Weigh?

One cubic metre of gravel typically weighs between 1.4 and 1.7 tonnes, with 1.5 tonnes a handy average for planning. Heavier crushed products and wet gravel can weigh more. Your supplier can give you the exact figure for the product you choose.

How to Estimate Gravel Delivery Costs

Delivery costs depend on the amount you order, the type of gravel, your distance from the supplier and how easily a truck can reach your site. Many suppliers charge a minimum delivery fee, and smaller loads can cost more per cubic metre than larger ones. To estimate your cost, multiply the quantity you need by the supplier’s price per cubic metre or tonne, then add the delivery fee. Ask for a quote so you know the full price before you commit.

Choosing the Right Gravel for Your Project

  • Driveways: crushed rock, road base or 20mm gravel for a firm, stable surface
  • Paths: pea gravel or smaller decomposed granite for a comfortable walking surface
  • Decorative areas: river pebbles, scoria or coloured stone for appearance
  • Drainage: clean, washed 20mm aggregate that allows water to flow through

For small jobs under half a cubic metre, bags are often easier. For anything larger, bulk delivery is usually better value and saves a lot of lifting.

Frequently Asked Questions About the Gravel Calculator

How Much Gravel Do I Need to Cover 1 Square Metre?+

It depends on the depth. At 50mm deep, 1m² needs 0.05m³, which is about 75kg of gravel. At 100mm deep, it needs 0.1m³, which is about 150kg. Multiply by the number of square metres to find your total.

How Many Cubic Metres of Gravel Do I Need?+

Multiply the length by the width by the depth, with all three in metres. For example, an area of 6m × 3m at 75mm deep needs 6 × 3 × 0.075 = 1.35m³. With the calculator’s default 15% combined compaction-and-waste allowance, 1.35m³ becomes 1.5525m³, displayed as about 1.55m³. You can choose a different allowance in the tool.

How Deep Should Gravel Be for a Driveway?+

A finished layer of 75 to 100mm is typical for a driveway. If the ground is soft or the driveway sees heavy vehicles, add a compacted base layer of 100 to 150mm beneath the top layer.

How Much Does 1m³ of Gravel Weigh?+

One cubic metre of gravel usually weighs between 1.4 and 1.7 tonnes. For quick planning, use 1.5 tonnes per cubic metre and confirm the exact weight with your supplier.

How Do I Calculate Gravel for an Irregular Area?+

Break the area into simple shapes such as rectangles, triangles and circles. Calculate the area of each shape, multiply each by the depth to get the volume, then add all the volumes together. For a curved garden bed, a close estimate using a few rectangles is usually accurate enough.

Should I Order Extra Gravel for My Project?+

The calculator starts with a combined 15% compaction-and-waste allowance. You can change it to 0%, 5%, 10%, 15% or a custom percentage to suit the material and site conditions.
