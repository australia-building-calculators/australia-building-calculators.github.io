How to Use the Lineal Metre Calculator

Enter the Required Measurement

Enter the measurement you already have. This could be an area in square metres, such as the size of a floor, or a length in lineal metres, such as the distance along a wall. Choose the correct unit so the calculator knows which conversion to apply.

Select the Material Width

Enter the width of the material you plan to buy, in metres or millimetres. This could be the width of a carpet roll, a decking board, a fabric roll or a timber profile. The width is what turns an area into a length, so it must be accurate.

Convert Square Metres into Lineal Metres

To find how much length you need to cover an area, the calculator divides the area by the width of the material. For example, 12m² of floor covered with a material that is 1.2m wide needs 10 lineal metres.

Convert Lineal Metres into Square Metres

To find how much area a length of material covers, the calculator multiplies the length by the width. For example, 10 lineal metres of 1.2m wide material covers 12m².

Calculate the Required Material Length

The final result is the total length you need to buy. Round up to the next whole metre or the next available length, and remember to add an allowance for waste.

What Is a Lineal Metre?

Understanding Lineal Metres and Linear Measurements

A lineal metre is a measurement of length. It is one metre measured along a line, regardless of the width or thickness of the material. A lineal metre of a narrow board and a lineal metre of a wide roll are the same length, but they cover very different areas.

What Is the Difference Between a Lineal Metre and a Metre?

There is no difference in actual length. A lineal metre is one metre. The word lineal is used by suppliers to make it clear that the price or quantity refers to length only, not area or volume.

Lineal Metres vs Square Metres

A lineal metre measures length in one direction. A square metre measures area, which is length multiplied by width. To convert between them, you must know the width of the material.

Lineal Metres vs Cubic Metres

A cubic metre measures volume, which is length, width and thickness together. Timber is often sold by the lineal metre but delivered or priced in cubic metres. To convert, you need both the width and thickness of the timber.

How to Calculate Lineal Metres

Lineal Metre Calculation Formula

The formulas are:

Lineal metres = Area (m²) ÷ Width (m)

Area (m²) = Lineal metres × Width (m)

Always convert the width to metres first. For example, 90mm becomes 0.09m.

Converting Square Metres into Lineal Metres

Divide the area by the width. For example, 20m² of material that is 2m wide needs 20 ÷ 2 = 10 lineal metres. A narrower material needs more length for the same area.

Converting Lineal Metres into Square Metres

Multiply the length by the width. For example, 15 lineal metres of 0.9m wide material covers 15 × 0.9 = 13.5m².

How Material Width Affects Lineal Metre Calculations

Width makes a big difference. To cover 10m², you need 10 lineal metres of 1m wide material, about 6.7 lineal metres of 1.5m wide material, or only about 2.7 lineal metres of 3.66m wide carpet. Always check the width before you buy.

Worked Examples of Lineal Metre Conversions

Example 1: Fabric. You need to cover 8m² with fabric that is 1.5m wide. Lineal metres = 8 ÷ 1.5 = 5.33, so buy about 5.5 lineal metres.

Example 2: Decking. You need to cover 12m² with 140mm boards and 5mm gaps. The effective width is 145mm, or 0.145m. Lineal metres = 12 ÷ 0.145 = 82.8, so buy about 83 lineal metres, plus extra for cuts.

Lineal Metres to Square Metres

Multiply the length by the width. For example, 50 lineal metres of 90mm decking covers 50 × 0.09 = 4.5m². If there are gaps between boards, use the effective width including the gap.

Converting Timber Measurements into Lineal Metres

Timber is usually sold in set lengths, such as 2.4m, 3.0m, 3.6m and 4.8m. To work out how many pieces you need, divide the total lineal metres by the length of each piece, and round up. For example, 20 lineal metres in 3.6m lengths needs 20 ÷ 3.6 = 5.6, so 6 pieces.

Understanding Cubic Metres and Lineal Metres for Timber

To convert cubic metres into lineal metres, divide the volume by the cross sectional area of the timber. For 90 × 45mm timber, the cross section is 0.09 × 0.045 = 0.00405m². So 1m³ of this timber is 1 ÷ 0.00405 = about 247 lineal metres.

Where Are Lineal Metres Used?

Lineal Metres in Timber and Building Materials

Framing timber, treated pine, architraves and many other building products are priced by the lineal metre. This makes it easy to compare prices between different lengths and suppliers.

Lineal Metres for Carpet and Flooring

Carpet is sold from rolls that are commonly 3.66m or 4m wide, and priced by the lineal metre of the roll. You pay for the length you need, across the full width of the roll, even if your room is narrower.

Lineal Metres for Fabric and Material Rolls

Fabric is sold by the lineal metre off a roll. Standard widths are around 1.4 to 1.5m for curtain and upholstery fabric, but they vary, so the width matters when you compare prices.

Lineal Metres for Skirting Boards and Trim

Skirting boards, cornices, architraves and trim are bought by the lineal metre. Measure the perimeter of the room, subtract door openings, and add 10% for mitre cuts and waste.

Lineal Metres for Fencing and Decking

Fence rails, palings, decking boards and edging are all sold by length. For fencing, measure the length of the fence line. For decking, convert the deck area into lineal metres of board using the board width and gap.

Practical Examples of Lineal Metre Calculations

Calculating Carpet Length from Floor Area

A bedroom measures 3.5m × 4.5m, and the carpet roll is 3.66m wide. The room is narrower than the roll, so you only need the length of the room: 4.5m. Add about 10 to 15cm for trimming, so order around 4.6m. The area calculation would give 15.75 ÷ 3.66 = 4.3m, but the room dimensions control what you actually buy.

Calculating Timber Length for a Building Project

A small stud wall is 4.8m long and 2.4m high, with studs at 450mm centres. You need about 4.8 ÷ 0.45 = 10.7, so 11 studs, plus 1 at the end, giving 12 studs. Each stud is 2.4m, so you need 12 × 2.4 = 28.8 lineal metres, plus top and bottom plates of 4.8m each, which adds another 9.6 lineal metres. The total is about 38.4 lineal metres.

Estimating Skirting Board Requirements for a Room

A room is 4m × 3.5m, so the perimeter is (4 + 3.5) × 2 = 15m. Subtract one 0.8m door opening to get 14.2m. Add 10% for cuts and waste, giving about 15.6 lineal metres. Skirting is usually sold in 5.4m lengths, so you would buy 3 lengths.

Calculating Material Requirements for Different Roll Widths

To cover 20m², you need 20 lineal metres of 1m wide material, 13.3 of 1.5m wide, or 10 of 2m wide. A wider roll reduces the length needed, but it may create more waste if your space does not match the roll width. Compare the total cost, not just the price per lineal metre.

What Factors Affect Lineal Metre Calculations?

Material Width and Available Sizes

Materials come in standard widths, so you cannot always choose the perfect size. Check what is available before you plan, and adjust the layout to suit the widths on offer.

Cutting Allowances and Material Wastage

Cuts, trims and offcuts mean you buy more than the theoretical amount. Allow 5 to 10% for simple jobs and 10 to 15% for complex layouts, patterned materials and diagonal cuts.

Joins, Overlaps and Installation Requirements

Some materials need overlaps or pattern matching. Patterned carpet and wallpaper need extra to line up the pattern. Overlapping materials, such as sheet flooring and membranes, need extra width at each join.

Understanding Manufacturer Measurement Specifications

Manufacturers often list nominal and actual sizes. A board sold as 90mm may measure slightly less, and a roll may be trimmed at the edges. Check the specifications and use the actual cover width in your calculations.

Frequently Asked Questions About the Lineal Metre Calculator

How Many Lineal Metres Are in One Metre?+

One. A lineal metre is simply one metre of length. The word lineal confirms that the measurement is of length only.

How Do I Convert Square Metres into Lineal Metres?+

Divide the area in square metres by the width of the material in metres. For example, 15m² of material that is 1.5m wide needs 15 ÷ 1.5 = 10 lineal metres.

How Many Lineal Metres Are in 10 Square Metres?+

It depends on the width. For material 1m wide, it is 10 lineal metres. At 1.2m wide it is about 8.3, and at 1.5m wide it is about 6.7. Always divide by the actual width.

Can You Convert Cubic Metres into Lineal Metres?+

Yes, if you know the cross section of the material. Divide the cubic metres by the width times the thickness, with both in metres. For 90 × 45mm timber, 1m³ is about 247 lineal metres.

Why Does Material Width Matter When Calculating Lineal Metres?+

Because width turns length into area. A wider material covers more area per lineal metre, so you need less length. A narrow material covers less, so you need more.

Are Lineal Metres and Linear Metres the Same?+

Yes. Lineal metre and linear metre both mean a metre of length. Lineal is the more common term in Australia, especially in the building and flooring trades.
