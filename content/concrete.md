## How Much Concrete Do You Need for Your Project?

### Concrete Requirements for Driveways and Paths

A driveway that is 12m long and 3m wide at 100mm thick needs 12 × 3 × 0.1 = 3.6m³. A footpath that is 10m long, 1m wide and 75mm thick needs 10 × 1 × 0.075 = 0.75m³. Add 5 to 10% to both for uneven ground and spillage.

### Concrete Volume for House Slabs and Floor Slabs

A house slab is more complex than a flat pad, as it has thickened edge beams and internal beams. For a rough estimate, calculate the main slab area at 100mm, then add the volume of the beams separately. For a real project, the engineer’s drawings will list the concrete volume. A 10m × 8m slab at 100mm is 8m³ before beams.

### Calculating Concrete for Square and Circular Footings

For a square footing that is 0.6m × 0.6m and 0.5m deep, the volume is 0.6 × 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.18m³. For a circular footing with a 0.45m diameter and 0.6m depth, the volume is π × 0.225 × 0.225 × 0.6 = 0.095m³. Multiply by the number of footings.

### Concrete Requirements for Fence Posts and Columns

Fence posts are usually set in holes that are 300mm in diameter and 600 to 900mm deep. Each 300mm × 600mm hole needs about 0.042m³ of concrete before the post takes up space. For 20 posts, that is about 0.85m³. Subtract the volume of the post itself for a more accurate figure.

### Calculating Concrete for Steps and Other Structures

For concrete steps, calculate each step as a separate rectangle and add them together. Each step is the width of the stairs, multiplied by the tread depth, multiplied by the height of the step from the ground. For irregular structures, break them into simple shapes.

## How to Calculate Concrete Volume

### Concrete Volume Formula: Length × Width × Thickness

The formula is:

**Volume (m³) = Length (m) × Width (m) × Thickness (m)**

All measurements must be in metres, and the result is in cubic metres.

### Calculating Concrete for Rectangular Slabs

Multiply the length by the width to get the area, then by the thickness. A slab of 6m × 4m × 0.1m needs 6 × 4 × 0.1 = 2.4m³. To convert millimetres to metres, divide by 1,000, so 100mm becomes 0.1m.

### Calculating Concrete for Circular Footings and Columns

The formula for a cylinder is:

**Volume (m³) = π × radius² × depth**

The radius is half the diameter, so a 300mm diameter hole has a radius of 0.15m. Using 0.15 × 0.15 × 3.14 × 0.6 gives about 0.042m³ per hole.

### Calculating Concrete for Multiple Structures

Calculate the volume of each different structure and add them together. If you have identical items, calculate one and multiply. Adding everything into a single order helps you avoid short load fees and multiple deliveries.

### Practical Concrete Calculation Example

Say you are pouring a patio slab that is 5m long and 4m wide, 100mm thick.

- Convert the thickness: 100mm = 0.1m

- Calculate the volume: 5 × 4 × 0.1 = **2m³**

- Add 10% for waste and uneven ground: 2 × 1.1 = **2.2m³**

- Estimate the weight: 2.2m³ × 2.4 tonnes per m³ = about **5.3 tonnes**

You would order around 2.2m³, or 2.5m³ if your supplier rounds up.

## How Thick Should a Concrete Slab Be?

### Concrete Thickness for Footpaths and Paths

Footpaths and garden paths are usually 75 to 100mm thick. They only carry foot traffic and light loads, so they do not need to be thicker. Use 100mm if wheelbarrows or mobility scooters will be used regularly.

### Concrete Thickness for Driveways

Residential driveways are typically 100 to 125mm thick, and 150mm for heavier vehicles such as trucks, caravans and campervans. Driveways are normally reinforced with mesh, and control joints are used to manage cracking.

### Concrete Thickness for Residential Slabs

A standard residential slab is usually around 100mm thick, with thicker edge and internal beams. The design depends on the soil classification at your site, and the engineer’s plans will state the thickness and reinforcement. Do not rely on a general rule for a house slab.

### Why Concrete Thickness Depends on Ground Conditions and Loads

Soft or reactive soils need thicker, more heavily reinforced slabs. Heavier loads, such as vehicles, also need more thickness. A slab on firm, well drained ground can be thinner than one on clay that swells and shrinks with the seasons.

## What Factors Affect Concrete Requirements?

### Ground Preparation and Subgrade Levels

A well compacted, level base uses concrete efficiently. Uneven or soft ground leads to variations in thickness, and you can use far more concrete than expected. Compact the base and add a layer of crushed rock if needed.

### Reinforcement and Structural Requirements

Reinforcing mesh, bars and cages do not change the volume much, but they affect the design and thickness. Structural slabs, retaining walls and footings need engineering input. Always follow the plans and building code requirements for your project.

### Formwork Dimensions and Slab Thickness

Formwork can bow outwards under the weight of wet concrete, which increases the volume. Use strong, well braced forms, and measure the actual internal dimensions after they are built, not the planned dimensions.

### Concrete Settlement, Spillage and Wastage

Some concrete is lost to spillage, ground absorption and leftover material in the chute or wheelbarrow. A margin of 5 to 10% is standard for most jobs. Allow more for uneven ground and complex shapes.

### Why Accurate Measurements Matter Before Ordering

Running out of concrete halfway through a pour creates cold joints, which weaken the slab. Ordering too much costs money and leaves you disposing of wet concrete. Measure carefully, double check your calculation, and confirm the figure with your supplier.

## Understanding Concrete Volume, Weight and Mix

### What Does One Cubic Metre of Concrete Mean?

One cubic metre is a block that is 1m long, 1m wide and 1m high. It will cover 10m² at 100mm thick, 20m² at 50mm, or about 13.3m² at 75mm. A standard concrete truck carries 5 to 8m³ depending on its size.

### Concrete Volume vs Concrete Weight

Concrete weighs about 2.4 tonnes per cubic metre, but you order it by volume, not weight. The weight matters when you are planning access, because a full truck is very heavy and can damage driveways and kerbs.

### Ready-Mix Concrete vs Mixing Concrete On-Site

Ready mix concrete is delivered wet and ready to pour. It has consistent quality, is fast and suits any pour over about 1m³. Mixing on site, using bagged premix or separate cement, sand and aggregate, suits small jobs such as fence posts, small pads and repairs.

### Understanding Concrete Mix Ratios

A common mix ratio for general work is 1 part cement, 2 parts sand and 3 parts coarse aggregate, by volume, with enough water for a workable mix. Ready mix concrete is specified by strength, such as N20 or N25 (20 or 25 megapascals), and your supplier can recommend the right grade.

### How Much Cement, Sand and Aggregate Are Needed?

For 1m³ of concrete using a 1:2:3 mix, you need roughly 350kg of cement (about 9 bags of 40kg), 0.5m³ of sand and 0.75m³ of aggregate. These are approximate figures, as they vary with the mix design and moisture. For the calculator’s starting bag yields, a 20kg bag is treated as about 10 litres (0.01m³), a 30kg bag as 15 litres (0.015m³), and a 40kg bag as 20 litres (0.02m³). That is about 100, 67 or 50 bags respectively for 1m³ before any allowance. Actual yields vary by product, so enter the yield printed on the bag.

## Ordering Concrete for Your Project

### How Many Cubic Metres of Concrete Should You Order?

Order the calculated volume plus 5 to 10%. For an uneven base or a complex shape, add up to 15%. The calculator rounds its suggested ready-mix quantity up in 0.2m³ steps. Your supplier may use different minimum loads or delivery increments, so confirm the final order directly.

### When Should You Order Ready-Mix Concrete?

Order ready mix for slabs, driveways, large footings and any pour over about 1m³. Book early in the week for weekend pours, as suppliers fill up fast. Avoid pouring in extreme heat or heavy rain, and plan the work for the cooler part of the day in summer.

### What to Consider Before Scheduling Concrete Delivery

Check that the truck can reach the site, that the access is wide and strong enough, and that you have enough people to place and finish the concrete. Have your formwork, reinforcement and base ready before the truck arrives, because wet concrete cannot wait.

### Why You May Need an Additional Concrete Allowance

An allowance protects against shortfalls caused by uneven ground, bowing forms and spillage. Many suppliers charge a short load fee for small orders, so ordering a little more can be better value than arranging a second delivery.

## Frequently Asked Questions About the Concrete Calculator

### How Much Concrete Do I Need for a 10m² Slab?

At 100mm thick, a 10m² slab needs 10 × 0.1 = 1m³ of concrete before allowance. With the calculator’s starting 10% waste allowance, the volume is 1.1m³. Its suggested ready-mix order rounds up in 0.2m³ steps, so the suggested order is **1.2m³**. At 75mm the base volume is 0.75m³, and at 150mm it is 1.5m³, before allowance.

### How Many Cubic Metres of Concrete Are in a Standard Slab?

It depends on the size. A 6m × 4m slab at 100mm needs 2.4m³. A 10m × 8m slab at 100mm needs 8m³, before beams. Use the calculator for your exact dimensions.

### How Much Does 1m³ of Concrete Weigh?

One cubic metre of concrete weighs about 2.4 tonnes, or roughly 2,400kg. Reinforced concrete is a little heavier.

### How Do I Calculate Concrete for Circular Footings?

Use the formula π × radius² × depth. For a footing with a 0.4m diameter and 0.6m depth, the radius is 0.2m, so the volume is 3.14 × 0.2 × 0.2 × 0.6 = 0.075m³. Multiply by the number of footings.

### How Much Extra Concrete Should I Order?

Order 5 to 10% extra for most jobs, and up to 15% for uneven ground or complex shapes. It is better to have a little left over than to run short halfway through a pour.

### Is Ready-Mix Concrete Better Than Mixing It Yourself?

For larger jobs, yes. Ready mix is consistent, saves labour and gives a stronger, more reliable result. Mixing yourself suits small jobs such as fence posts or minor repairs, where the quantity is small and the work is not structural.
