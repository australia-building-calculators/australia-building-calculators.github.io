## How to Use the Mulch Calculator

### Measure the Length and Width of Your Garden Bed

Use a tape measure to record the length and width of the bed in metres. For curved beds, measure the widest and narrowest points and use the average, or split the bed into simple shapes. If you are mulching around trees, measure the diameter of the circle you want to cover.

### Enter Your Desired Mulch Depth

Enter the depth in millimetres. For most garden beds, 50 to 75mm is a good depth. The calculator converts millimetres to metres for you, so you do not need to do the maths yourself.

### Calculate the Required Mulch Volume

As you enter or change valid measurements, the result updates automatically. The tool multiplies length, width and depth to give the volume in cubic metres. This is the amount you need to buy, and it is how bulk mulch is sold.

### Estimate the Number of Mulch Bags Needed

If you are buying bagged mulch, choose 25L, 30L, 40L or 50L bags, or enter a custom size. One cubic metre is 1,000 litres, so a 50L bag holds 0.05m³. The number of bags is rounded up to a whole bag.

## How Much Mulch Do You Need?

### Mulch Requirements for Garden Beds

A garden bed that is 5m long and 1.5m wide at 75mm deep needs 5 × 1.5 × 0.075 = 0.56m³ of mulch. Adding 10% for settling brings that to about 0.62m³. If you are topping up existing mulch, calculate only the extra depth you need.

### How Much Mulch Do You Need Around Trees and Shrubs?

A ring of mulch around a tree helps retain moisture and suppress weeds. For a circle with a radius of 1m, the area is about 3.14m². At 75mm deep, that needs 3.14 × 0.075 = 0.24m³. Larger trees can use wider rings, as the roots spread well beyond the trunk.

### Mulch Requirements for Landscaping Areas

For larger landscaped areas such as playground surfaces, pathways and open garden spaces, the same formula applies. Coarse, chunky mulches may need a greater depth, around 75 to 100mm, to suppress weeds and stay in place.

### Calculating Mulch for Multiple Garden Beds

Add each bed as a separate area when all beds use the same depth. The calculator applies one depth, allowance and bag size to every area in that calculation. If beds need different depths, run separate calculations and add the volumes. Ordering the combined amount in one delivery can be cheaper than several small orders.

### How to Calculate Mulch for Circular and Irregular Areas

For circles, use π × radius² for the area. For irregular beds, split the shape into rectangles, triangles and circles, calculate each area, add them together, and multiply by the depth. A rough estimate is usually enough for mulch, because it is forgiving and easy to spread.

## How to Calculate Mulch Volume

### Mulch Calculation Formula: Length × Width × Depth

The formula is:

**Volume (m³) = Length (m) × Width (m) × Depth (m)**

All measurements must be in metres, and the result is in cubic metres.

### Converting Mulch Depth from Millimetres to Metres

Divide the depth in millimetres by 1,000. For example, 50mm = 0.05m, 75mm = 0.075m and 100mm = 0.1m. Entering 75 instead of 0.075 makes the result 1,000 times too large, which is a very common mistake.

### Calculating Mulch in Cubic Metres

Once you have the volume, you can convert to other measures. One cubic metre equals 1,000 litres. A 50 litre bag holds 0.05m³, so one cubic metre is about 20 bags. A 25 litre bag holds 0.025m³, so one cubic metre is about 40 bags.

### Worked Example of a Mulch Calculation

Let’s say you want to mulch a garden bed that is 6m long and 2m wide at a depth of 75mm.

- Convert the depth: 75mm = 0.075m

- Calculate the volume: 6 × 2 × 0.075 = **0.9m³**

- Add 10% for settling: 0.9 × 1.1 = **0.99m³**, or about 1m³

- Convert to bags: 1m³ ÷ 0.05m³ per 50L bag = **20 bags**

You would order 1 cubic metre of bulk mulch, or around 20 bags of 50 litres.

## What Is the Recommended Mulch Depth?

### Mulch Depth for Garden Beds

A depth of 50 to 75mm works well for most garden beds. It is thick enough to hold moisture and block weeds, but thin enough to let water and air reach the soil. Finer mulches can be applied a little thinner, while coarse mulches need a little more.

### Mulch Depth Around Trees and Shrubs

Use 75 to 100mm around trees and larger shrubs. A deeper layer helps keep roots cool in hot Australian summers and reduces the need for watering. Spread the mulch out to the drip line (the edge of the leaves) for the best effect.

### Mulch Depth for Weed Suppression

For good weed control, apply at least 75mm. Thinner layers allow light through and weeds will push up. Laying cardboard or weed mat under the mulch improves suppression, particularly on persistent weeds.

### Why Mulch Should Not Be Piled Against Plant Stems or Tree Trunks

Mulch piled against stems holds moisture against the bark, which can cause rot, fungal disease and pest problems. Keep mulch about 50 to 100mm away from stems and trunks, forming a shallow donut shape rather than a volcano.

## Choosing the Right Mulch for Your Garden

### Organic Mulch and Its Benefits

Organic mulches, made from plant material, break down over time and feed the soil. They improve soil structure, support worms and microbes, and help retain moisture. They need topping up every year or two as they decompose.

### Wood Chip and Bark Mulch

Wood chip and bark are popular choices for garden beds and trees. They last well, look natural and suppress weeds. Coarse bark lasts longer, while finer chips break down faster. Pine bark is common in Australia and suits many gardens.

### Compost and Other Organic Mulches

Compost, sugar cane mulch, pea straw, lucerne and leaf mulch are good for vegetable gardens and soil improvement. They break down quickly and add nutrients, so they suit beds that are replanted often. Keep straw type mulches a little thinner and away from stems.

### Decorative Mulch for Landscaping

Coloured mulches, pebbles and crushed stone are used for appearance rather than soil health. They suit feature areas and low maintenance gardens. Stone mulch does not decompose, but it can heat up in summer, so avoid it close to delicate plants.

### Choosing Mulch Based on Plant and Garden Requirements

Native gardens usually do well with coarse, low nutrient mulches such as bark or wood chip. Vegetable gardens and annuals benefit from fine, nutrient rich mulches such as compost or sugar cane. Match the mulch to what you are growing.

## What Factors Affect the Amount of Mulch You Need?

### Garden Bed Size and Shape

The area and shape of the bed set the base quantity. Beds with curves and tight corners are harder to measure, so use a simple average and add a small allowance.

### Existing Mulch and Ground Conditions

If there is already mulch, measure its depth and only add the difference. Rake it first to break up any compacted layer. Bare, dry soil may absorb some of the mulch’s moisture, so water the soil first.

### Mulch Type, Texture and Settlement

Fine mulches settle and pack down more than coarse ones. Fresh mulch can shrink by 10 to 20% as it settles and breaks down. Expect the layer to look thinner after a few months.

### Different Mulch Depths for Different Applications

A vegetable bed may need only 50mm of fine mulch, while a tree ring may need 100mm of coarse bark. Calculate each area separately for accurate numbers.

## Buying Mulch: Bags vs Bulk Delivery

The optional vehicle selector estimates loads by volume. Its starting capacities are 0.8m³ for a ute tray, 1.0m³ for a small trailer, 1.5m³ for a medium trailer and 2.0m³ for a large trailer. These are editable planning assumptions, not vehicle payload ratings. Mulch weight varies with type and moisture, so check the vehicle’s payload and the trailer’s towing limits before loading.

### How Many Bags of Mulch Make One Cubic Metre?

One cubic metre is 1,000 litres. You need about 40 bags of 25L mulch, 34 bags of 30L mulch, 25 bags of 40L mulch or 20 bags of 50L mulch. The calculator also accepts a custom size. Check the volume printed on the bag.

### How Much Does a Cubic Metre of Mulch Weigh?

Mulch is light compared to soil or gravel. A cubic metre of bark or wood chip typically weighs between 300 and 500kg, depending on the type and how wet it is. Wet mulch is noticeably heavier.

### Is Bulk Mulch Cheaper Than Bagged Mulch?

Usually, yes. Bulk mulch costs much less per cubic metre than bags, especially for larger areas. Bags are handy for small jobs, top ups and when you cannot take a delivery, but they get expensive quickly.

### How to Estimate Your Mulch Delivery Requirements

Use the calculator to find the volume, add 10%, and round up to the nearest half or full cubic metre. Check where the truck can drop the load, and use a tarp or a clear area, as bulk mulch is delivered in a pile.

## Frequently Asked Questions About the Mulch Calculator

### How Much Mulch Do I Need for 1 Square Metre?

At 50mm deep, 1m² needs 0.05m³, which is 50 litres. At 75mm, it needs 0.075m³ (75 litres). Multiply by the number of square metres to find your total.

### How Many Cubic Metres of Mulch Do I Need?

Multiply the length by the width by the depth, with all measurements in metres. A bed of 4m × 2m at 75mm needs 4 × 2 × 0.075 = 0.6m³. Add 10% for settling.

### How Deep Should Mulch Be in a Garden Bed?

Aim for 50 to 75mm in most garden beds, and 75 to 100mm around trees and for weed control. Keep it away from stems and trunks.

### How Many Bags of Mulch Do I Need?

Divide the volume in litres by the bag size. For example, 0.6m³ is 600 litres, which needs 12 bags of 50L, or 24 bags of 25L.

### When Is the Best Time to Apply Mulch?

In most of Australia, spring and autumn are good times. Apply mulch after rain, when the soil is moist and warm, and before the heat of summer. Water the soil first if it is dry.

### Can I Put New Mulch Over Existing Mulch?

Yes, but rake the old mulch first to loosen it. Check the depth, and only add enough to bring it back to 50 to 75mm. Too much mulch can suffocate roots and hold excess moisture.
