How to Use the Screed Calculator

Enter the Length and Width of Your Floor

Measure the length and width of the floor in metres. For rooms that are not rectangular, split the floor into rectangles, measure each one and calculate them separately. Measure the area to be screeded, not the overall room if parts of the floor will not be covered.

Select the Required Screed Thickness

Enter the thickness in millimetres. The right thickness depends on the type of screed and whether it is bonded, unbonded or floating. As a guide, bonded screeds are 25 to 40mm, while floating screeds are 65mm or more. The calculator converts the thickness to metres for you.

Calculate the Floor Area and Screed Volume

As you enter or change valid measurements, the result updates automatically. The tool multiplies length, width and thickness to give the volume of screed in cubic metres. It also shows the floor area in square metres.

Estimate the Total Screed Material Required

The result shows finished screed volume including the entered allowance. Choose the screed type to see the corresponding estimate: cement bags and sand tonnes for a sand-and-cement mix, volume for liquid screed, or ready-mix bag count based on the entered litres per bag.

Calculate Screed Requirements for Multiple Rooms

If you are screeding more than one room, calculate each one separately and add the volumes together. This makes it easy to plan one delivery or one batch of materials.

How Much Screed Do You Need for Your Floor?

Calculating Screed for Residential Floors

A living room that is 5m × 4m with 50mm of screed needs 5 × 4 × 0.05 = 1m³ before allowance. With the calculator’s starting 10% allowance, the estimated screed volume is 1.1m³. The material quantities then depend on whether you select sand-and-cement, liquid or bagged screed.

Screed Requirements for Bathrooms and Wet Areas

Bathrooms often need screed laid to falls, so water runs towards the drain. This means the thickness varies across the floor. Calculate using the average thickness. A 2.5m × 2m bathroom with an average thickness of 60mm needs 2.5 × 2 × 0.06 = 0.3m³. Waterproofing is a separate, essential step before tiling.

Calculating Screed for Large Floor Areas

For large areas such as open plan living spaces and garages, the volume adds up quickly. A 10m × 8m floor at 50mm needs 4m³, or about 8 tonnes. Large pours may be better with a ready mixed or pumped screed to keep the surface consistent.

Screed Requirements for Uneven Floors

Uneven floors need more screed in the low spots. Check the floor with a long straight edge or a laser level, find the highest and lowest points, and use the average depth. If the variation is large, use a levelling compound or a thicker screed.

Calculating Screed for Multiple Floor Levels

If the floor is on different levels, calculate each level as a separate area with its own thickness, then add the volumes. Pay attention to the transitions between levels, as these need extra care and sometimes a different thickness.

How to Calculate Screed Volume

Screed Calculation Formula: Length × Width × Thickness

The formula is:

Volume (m³) = Length (m) × Width (m) × Thickness (m)

All measurements must be in metres, and the result is in cubic metres.

Converting Screed Thickness from Millimetres to Metres

Divide the thickness in millimetres by 1,000. For example, 40mm = 0.04m, 50mm = 0.05m, and 75mm = 0.075m. A very common mistake is entering the thickness in millimetres without converting.

Calculating Screed Volume in Cubic Metres

Once you have the volume, you can work out the weight by multiplying by the density. Sand and cement screed weighs roughly 2 tonnes per cubic metre. At 50mm thick, each square metre needs 0.05m³, which is about 100kg.

Worked Example of a Floor Screed Calculation

Say you are screeding a floor that is 4m long and 3m wide, at a thickness of 50mm.

  • Convert the thickness: 50mm = 0.05m
  • Calculate the area: 4 × 3 = 12m²
  • Calculate the volume: 12 × 0.05 = 0.6m³
  • Add 10% for variation and waste: 0.6 × 1.1 = 0.66m³
  • Estimate the weight: 0.66 × 2 = about 1.3 tonnes

For the calculator’s sand-and-cement option, the starting mix ratio is 4 parts sand to 1 part cement and the cement bag selector starts at 20kg. For a 4m × 3m × 50mm floor with a 10% allowance, the tool estimates about 13 bags of 20kg cement and about 1.1 tonnes of sand. If you select bagged ready-mix instead, the starting yield is 12 litres per bag, giving 55 bags for 0.66m³; check and edit the actual bag yield.

What Is the Recommended Screed Thickness?

Typical Screed Thickness for Different Applications

The right thickness depends on the screed type and how it is laid. The table below shows typical ranges. Always follow the manufacturer’s instructions for the product you choose.

Screed typeTypical thickness
Bonded sand and cement25 to 40mm
Unbonded sand and cement40 to 50mm or more
Floating sand and cement65 to 75mm
Liquid or flowing screed25 to 50mm, depending on the product

These are general ranges. Specific products and project specifications can differ.

Bonded Screed vs Unbonded Screed

A bonded screed is stuck directly to the concrete base using a bonding agent. It can be thinner, because it works together with the base. An unbonded screed is separated from the base by a membrane, so it must be thicker to stand on its own.

Floating Screed and Its Thickness Requirements

A floating screed sits on top of insulation or acoustic layers, and is not attached to the base. It needs to be thicker, typically 65mm or more, because it has to carry loads without support from the base. It is common in upper floors and over insulation.

Screed Thickness for Underfloor Heating

With underfloor heating, the screed covers the pipes or cables. A sand and cement screed over heating usually needs 65 to 75mm in total, with a minimum cover over the pipes. Liquid screeds can be thinner, often 30 to 50mm, because they flow around the pipes. Always follow the heating and screed manufacturers’ guidance.

Why Screed Thickness Depends on the Screed System

Each screed system is designed for a specific thickness range. Using too little can cause cracking and curling, and using too much wastes material and slows drying. Check the product data sheet before you finalise the thickness.

Types of Floor Screed and Their Applications

Traditional Sand and Cement Screed

This is the most common type, mixed from sand and cement, and sometimes with an additive. The calculator offers sand-to-cement ratios of 3:1, 4:1 and 5:1, with 4:1 selected initially. Choose the mix specified for your screed product or project; these ratios are not structural design advice. It is cost effective and durable, but it takes longer to dry and needs skill to lay and level.

Liquid and Flowing Screed

Liquid screed is a pourable mix, usually pumped into place. It flows into corners and around pipes, and gives a very flat surface with less labour. It is popular over underfloor heating and for large areas.

Self-Levelling Screed

Self levelling compounds are thin, pourable products used to correct small variations before laying floor coverings. They are usually applied at 3 to 10mm, and are not a substitute for a structural screed. They are ideal for smoothing an existing slab.

Fast-Drying Screed

Fast drying screeds contain special binders and additives, so floor coverings can be laid in days instead of weeks. They are useful when time is short, but they cost more and need careful handling.

Choosing the Right Screed for Your Flooring Project

Choose based on the thickness you need, the flooring that will go on top, whether you have underfloor heating, and how quickly you need to finish. Ask your flooring supplier or installer for advice, as some floor coverings have strict moisture limits.

What Factors Affect Screed Material Requirements?

Floor Area and Average Screed Depth

The floor area and the average depth set the base volume. Even a few millimetres of extra depth across a large floor adds a lot of material.

Uneven Subfloors and Variations in Thickness

If the subfloor is uneven, the screed fills the low spots and uses more material. Measure the floor at several points to find the average depth, rather than using the thinnest part.

Screed Type and Material Density

Different screeds have different densities. Sand and cement screed is around 2 tonnes per cubic metre, and some lightweight or liquid screeds differ. Check the product data for the weight and yield.

Underfloor Heating and Insulation Layers

Insulation and heating pipes change the total build up of the floor. They affect the thickness of screed, the floor height and the doorway clearances. Plan the full build up before you order.

Material Waste and Application Conditions

Some screed is lost to spillage, mixing and trimming. Hot or windy weather can also cause problems, as screed dries too quickly and may crack. Allow 5 to 10% for waste, and plan to work in good conditions.

Preparing Your Floor Before Applying Screed

Cleaning and Preparing the Subfloor

Remove dust, dirt, oil, paint and loose material from the base. A clean surface is essential for a good bond. For concrete slabs, sweep and vacuum, and remove any contamination.

Checking Floor Levels and Required Depth

Use a laser level or a long straight edge to check the floor. Mark the finished floor level on the walls, and check that the screed thickness is enough at the thinnest point. Fix any big dips before you start.

Using Primer and Bonding Agents

Bonded screeds need a bonding agent or slurry to stick to the base. Primers can also control suction on porous bases. Follow the screed manufacturer’s recommendations for the product to use.

Installing Insulation and Underfloor Heating

Lay insulation boards and edge strips first, then install the heating pipes or cables as per the design. Pressure test any heating pipes before screeding, and keep them under pressure while the screed is laid.

Understanding Drying and Curing Requirements

Screed needs to cure slowly. Protect it from drafts, direct sun and rain, and keep traffic off until it has set. After curing, it must dry to the moisture level required by the flooring supplier before coverings go on.

Ordering and Mixing Screed

For the sand-and-cement option, the calculator applies a 30% dry-material volume factor to the estimated screed volume, then splits that dry volume using the selected sand-to-cement ratio. Its starting assumptions are a cement bulk density of 1,440kg/m³ and sand density of 1.6 tonnes/m³. These are estimating assumptions; actual mix design, moisture and product yield can differ. The 30% factor is not used to calculate liquid screed or bagged ready-mix bag counts.

Bagged Screed vs Ready-Mixed Screed

Bagged screed suits small rooms and repairs. It is easy to handle, but mixing and laying takes time. Ready mixed or pumped screed is better for large areas, because it is consistent and fast. Compare the price and the minimum order.

How Much Screed Is Needed per Square Metre?

It depends on the thickness. At 40mm, 1m² needs 0.04m³, or about 80kg. At 50mm, it needs 0.05m³, or about 100kg. At 75mm, it needs 0.075m³, or about 150kg.

Understanding Screed Coverage and Material Yield

For bagged ready-mix, enter the litres of finished screed yield stated on the bag. The calculator starts at 12 litres per bag and divides the estimated volume by that yield, rounding up to whole bags. Product yields vary, so replace the default with the manufacturer’s figure.

Why You Should Allow for Variations in Floor Depth

Floors are almost never perfectly flat. Allowing 10% extra means you will not run out when you find low spots, and you avoid joins that weaken the floor.

Frequently Asked Questions About the Screed Calculator

How Much Screed Do I Need per Square Metre?+

At 50mm thick, you need 0.05m³ per square metre, which is about 100kg. At 40mm you need about 80kg, and at 75mm about 150kg. Multiply by the floor area and add 10% for variations.

How Many Cubic Metres of Screed Do I Need?+

Multiply the length by the width by the thickness, with all measurements in metres. For example, a 6m × 4m floor at 50mm thick needs 6 × 4 × 0.05 = 1.2m³. Add 10%, so order about 1.3m³.

What Is the Minimum Screed Thickness?+

It depends on the type. Bonded sand and cement screeds can be as thin as 25mm, unbonded screeds are usually at least 40 to 50mm, and floating screeds are typically 65mm or more. Always follow the product and project specifications.

How Thick Should Screed Be Over Underfloor Heating?+

Sand and cement screed over heating is usually 65 to 75mm in total, while liquid screeds can be thinner, often 30 to 50mm over the pipes. Follow the guidance of both the heating and screed manufacturers.

How Much Does 1m³ of Screed Weigh?+

Sand and cement screed weighs around 2 tonnes per cubic metre. The exact weight depends on the mix and moisture, so check with your supplier.

How Long Does Floor Screed Take to Dry?+

A common rule of thumb for traditional sand and cement screed is about one day per millimetre of thickness up to 50mm, and longer beyond that. Fast drying and liquid screeds can be much quicker. Always check the manufacturer’s guidance and the moisture requirements of your flooring.

What Is the Difference Between Screed and Concrete?+

Screed is a fine mix of sand and cement, usually with little or no coarse aggregate. It is used as a smooth, level layer on top of a base. Concrete includes coarse aggregate, and is used for structural elements such as slabs, footings and driveways.
