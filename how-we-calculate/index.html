How we calculate

Every calculator uses simple, visible maths. Here are the formulas and typical values behind each one.

The basic approach

Most material calculators multiply measured areas by a depth or thickness to find volume in cubic metres. Some then convert that volume into tonnes, bags, boxes or litres using a typical density, bag yield or product coverage. Where a tool has a shared depth, tile size or allowance, that setting applies to each area entered in that calculator.

All measurements are metric. Results are rounded up where you would need to buy whole items, such as bags, tiles or tins.

Weights, bag yields, coverage rates and allowances are typical starting values. Where the calculator shows one in a box, you can change it to match your supplier specification.

Assumptions used

CalculatorWhat it uses
ConcreteVolume depends on the selected structure type (slab, square footing, circular footing or post hole, strip footing, column, wall or steps), plus the entered waste allowance (10% initially). Bag options are 20, 30 and 40 kg; default yields are 10, 15 and 20 litres respectively, and the yield can be edited. Concrete density starts at 2.4 tonnes per m³ and can be edited. The suggested ready-mix order is rounded up in 0.2 m³ steps.
GravelThe calculator adds one combined compaction-and-waste allowance (15% initially; the selector also offers 0%, 5%, 10%, 15% or a custom percentage). Typical densities are pebbles or 20 mm gravel 1.5 t/m³, crushed rock or blue metal 1.6 t/m³, sand 1.6 t/m³, crusher dust 1.7 t/m³ and road base 1.9 t/m³. The chosen density can be edited. Optional bag counts use the calculated weight and a 20 kg, 25 kg or custom bag weight.
SoilVolume plus a settling allowance (10% initially; selectable as 0%, 5%, 10%, 15% or custom). Starting densities are topsoil 1.3 t/m³, garden mix 1.1, lawn underlay 1.4, vegetable garden mix 1.0, compost 0.8, sandy soil 1.5 and fill soil 1.6; the density can be edited. Bag sizes are 25, 30, 40 or 50 litres, or a custom size. The suggested bulk order is rounded up to 0.5 m³ steps.
MulchVolume plus a settling allowance (10% initially; selectable as 0%, 5%, 10%, 15% or custom). Bags can be 25, 30, 40 or 50 litres, or a custom size. Optional load estimates start at 0.8 m³ for a ute tray, 1.0 m³ for a small trailer, 1.5 m³ for a medium trailer and 2.0 m³ for a large trailer; these are editable volume assumptions, not payload or towing ratings.
PaintWall area is each room perimeter times wall height, minus the entered door and window counts multiplied by their average areas (defaults: door 1.6 m², window 1.5 m²). Interior coverage starts at 12 m²/L and exterior coverage at 10 m²/L; both are editable. The surface-condition selector adds 10% for rough or textured surfaces and 20% for porous or unpainted surfaces. Topcoat count is editable. Optional primer uses its own editable coverage and coat count. Suggested tin sizes are 15, 10, 4, 2 and 1 litres.
TileThe tile face dimensions are each increased by the entered grout gap before tile quantity is calculated. The pattern selector starts at 10% for straight lay and brick bond, 15% for diagonal and 30% for herringbone; the cuts-and-breakage allowance remains editable. One shared tile size and grout gap apply to all entered areas, and the opening deduction is subtracted from their combined area. Adhesive and grout are optional estimates; the grout estimate uses 1.6 kg per litre and is indicative only.
BrickThe presets are a standard 230 × 110 × 76 mm brick, a double-height 230 × 110 × 162 mm brick and a 390 × 190 mm besser block. The mortar joint starts at 10 mm and is editable; this gives about 48.45 standard brick faces per m² per skin. Each wall has an opening-area deduction in m². The single/double-skin selector multiplies the face quantity by one or two. The optional mortar result estimates joint volume only and does not include a waste allowance.
ScreedThe calculator adds the selected waste and level-variation allowance (10% initially). For sand-and-cement screed it applies a 1.3 dry-material factor, splits that volume using the selected 3:1, 4:1 or 5:1 sand-to-cement ratio, estimates cement using 1,440 kg/m³ and sand using 1.6 t/m³, and counts 20 kg or 25 kg cement bags. Liquid screed is shown by volume. Bagged ready-mix uses the editable bag yield, which starts at 12 litres per bag.
Square metreArea is length × width, or the matching formula for a triangle or circle. One square metre is 10.764 square feet.
Cubic metreVolume is length × width × height, or the matching formula for a cylinder. One cubic metre is 1,000 litres.
Lineal metreFor area-to-length conversion, lineal metres equal area divided by the entered cover width, plus the optional 10% allowance (applies to this direction only). For length-to-area conversion, area equals length multiplied by width. For timber volume conversion, lineal metres equal volume divided by width and thickness. Material presets are starting widths; the width and its unit can be changed. Carpet layout, seams, board gaps and stock-length cutting plans are not automatically optimised.

Why your numbers may differ

Bag yields, material weights, paint coverage and tile sizes change between brands and suppliers. Wet or compacted materials weigh more. Uneven ground and cutting waste also use extra material.

Always check the product label and confirm quantities with your supplier before you order. These calculators are a planning guide only and are not engineering or trade advice.
