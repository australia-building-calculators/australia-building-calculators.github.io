How we calculate

Every calculator uses simple, visible maths. Here are the formulas and typical values behind each one.

The basic approach

Most calculators multiply length, width and depth to find a volume in cubic metres. Some then convert that volume into tonnes, bags or boxes using a typical material weight or yield.

All measurements are metric. Results are rounded up where you would need to buy whole items, such as bags, tiles or tins.

Weights, bag yields, coverage rates and allowances are typical starting values. Where the calculator shows one in a box, you can change it to match your supplier specification.

Assumptions used

CalculatorWhat it uses
ConcreteVolume depends on the structure type (slab, footing, post hole, column, wall or steps), plus your waste allowance. A 20 kg bag is assumed to make about 10 litres, a 30 kg bag 15 litres and a 40 kg bag 20 litres, and you can enter your own bag yield. Concrete density defaults to 2.4 tonnes per m³ and can be edited. Ready mix is rounded up to 0.2 m³ steps.
GravelVolume plus a compaction allowance (15% by default). Typical weight per m³: pebbles 1.5 t, crushed rock or blue metal 1.6 t, crusher dust 1.7 t, road base 1.9 t, sand 1.6 t. The density used is shown and can be edited. Bags are calculated from the weight and the bag size you choose.
SoilVolume plus a settling allowance (10% by default). Typical weight per m³: topsoil 1.3 t, garden mix 1.1 t, lawn underlay 1.4 t, vegetable garden mix 1.0 t, compost 0.8 t, sandy soil 1.5 t, fill soil 1.6 t. Densities can be edited. The order quantity is rounded up to 0.5 m³ steps.
MulchVolume plus an extra allowance (10% by default). Bags can be 25, 30, 40 or 50 litres or a custom size. Load capacities are typical values for a ute tray and for small, medium and large trailers, and you can edit them.
PaintWall area is each room perimeter times wall height, minus doors and windows. Doors default to 1.6 m² and windows to 1.5 m². Coverage defaults to 12 m² per litre for interior paint and 10 m² per litre for exterior paint, as a general assumption only. Rough surfaces add 10% and porous or unpainted surfaces add 20%. Tin sizes used: 15, 10, 4, 2 and 1 litre.
TileEach tile is measured with its grout gap included. Allowance defaults to 10% for straight lay or brick bond, 15% for diagonal and 30% for herringbone, and can be edited. Adhesive coverage and grout use are optional estimates, and grout is assumed to weigh 1.6 kg per litre.
BrickA standard Australian brick is 230 × 110 × 76 mm, a double height brick is 230 × 110 × 162 mm and a besser block is 390 × 190 mm. The mortar joint defaults to 10 mm, which gives about 48.45 standard bricks per m². Custom sizes and joint thickness can be entered. Double skin walls use twice the bricks.
ScreedDry materials take up about 30% more space than the finished screed. Cement is counted in 20 kg or 25 kg bags at 1,440 kg per m³, and sand at 1.6 tonnes per m³. Liquid screed and bagged ready mixed screed are calculated by volume or by bag yield.
Square metreArea is length × width, or the matching formula for a triangle or circle. One square metre is 10.764 square feet.
Cubic metreVolume is length × width × height, or the matching formula for a cylinder. One cubic metre is 1,000 litres.
Lineal metreLineal metres equal the area divided by the cover width, plus your extra allowance. Timber volume conversion divides the volume by the width and the thickness. Board and roll widths are common sizes and can be edited.

Why your numbers may differ

Bag yields, material weights, paint coverage and tile sizes change between brands and suppliers. Wet or compacted materials weigh more. Uneven ground and cutting waste also use extra material.

Always check the product label and confirm quantities with your supplier before you order. These calculators are a planning guide only and are not engineering or trade advice.
