How we calculate

Every calculator uses simple, visible maths. Here are the formulas and typical values behind each one.

The basic approach

Most calculators multiply length, width and depth to find a volume in cubic metres. Some then convert that volume into tonnes, bags or boxes using a typical material weight or yield.

All measurements are metric. Results are rounded up where you would need to buy whole items, such as bags, tiles or tins.

Assumptions used

CalculatorWhat it uses
ConcreteThe area is section 1 plus an optional second rectangle, multiplied by thickness, then adjusted for your waste allowance. A 20 kg bag is about 0.01 m³ and a 40 kg bag about 0.02 m³. Concrete weighs about 2.4 tonnes per m³.
GravelThe two optional rectangular areas are combined before multiplying by depth and adding a compaction allowance (15% by default). Weight per m³: pebbles 1.5 t, crushed rock or blue metal 1.6 t, crusher dust 1.7 t, road base 1.9 t, sand 1.6 t.
SoilThe two optional rectangular garden areas are combined before multiplying by depth and adding a settling allowance (10% by default). Weight per m³: topsoil 1.3 t, garden mix 1.1 t, compost 0.8 t. Bags are counted as 30 litres.
MulchThe two optional rectangular garden-bed areas are combined before multiplying by depth and adding an extra allowance (10% by default). Bags are counted as 30 litres.
PaintPaintable area is wall area minus doors and windows, plus any ceiling. Doors default to 1.6 m² and windows to 1.5 m², and you can change both. Coverage defaults to 12 m² per litre. Tin sizes used: 15, 10, 4, 2 and 1 litre.
TileThe two optional rectangular floor or wall areas are combined. Each tile is measured with its grout gap included. The result is rounded up and includes your cutting allowance (10% by default).
BrickA standard Australian brick is 230 × 110 × 76 mm and a besser block is 390 × 190 mm, both with a 10 mm joint. That gives about 48.45 bricks or 12.5 blocks per m². A brick course is 86 mm high and a block course is 200 mm.
ScreedDry materials take up about 30% more space than the finished screed. Cement is counted in 20 kg bags at 1,440 kg per m³, and sand at 1.6 tonnes per m³.
Square metreRectangle area is length × width; triangle area is base × perpendicular height ÷ 2; circle area is π × radius². A rectangle can include a second non-overlapping section. One square metre is 10.764 square feet.
Cubic metreRectangular volume is length × width × height. Cylinder volume is π × radius² × height, using half the entered diameter as the radius. One cubic metre is 1,000 litres.
Lineal metreLineal metres equal the area divided by the board or roll width, plus your extra allowance.

Why your numbers may differ

Bag yields, material weights, paint coverage and tile sizes change between brands and suppliers. Wet or compacted materials weigh more. Uneven ground and cutting waste also use extra material.

Always check the product label and confirm quantities with your supplier before you order. These calculators are a planning guide only and are not engineering or trade advice.
