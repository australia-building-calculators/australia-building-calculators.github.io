How to Use the Soil Calculator

Measure the Length and Width of Your Garden Area

Use a tape measure to record the length and width of the area in metres. For a rectangular bed, this is straightforward. For curved or odd shaped beds, split the area into simple shapes such as rectangles, then measure each one.

Enter the Required Soil Depth

Enter how deep the soil needs to be. Depth is usually quoted in millimetres, and the calculator converts it to metres automatically. A lawn needs roughly 100 to 150mm of topsoil, while garden beds and vegetable gardens need 200 to 300mm.

Calculate the Soil Volume in Cubic Metres

As you enter or change valid measurements, the result updates automatically. The tool multiplies length, width and depth to give the volume in cubic metres. It also gives an approximate weight, which is useful when you are arranging delivery or deciding how much you can move by trailer.

Calculate Soil Requirements for Multiple Garden Beds

Add each bed as a separate area when the beds share the same depth and soil type. The calculator applies one depth and one selected density to all areas in a single calculation. If beds need different depths or different soil types, run separate calculations and add the results.

How Much Soil Do You Need for Your Garden?

Calculating Soil for Raised Garden Beds

Raised beds are filled to the full height of the frame. A bed that is 2.4m long, 1.2m wide and 0.3m deep needs 2.4 × 1.2 × 0.3 = 0.864m³. Because the soil will settle, add 10 to 15% and plan for around 1m³. Fill to just below the top edge so water does not run over the sides.

How Much Topsoil Do You Need for a New Lawn?

A new lawn, whether from turf or seed, needs a layer of good quality topsoil. As a guide, spread 100 to 150mm of topsoil, and avoid going below about 75mm. A lawn that is 10m × 8m at 100mm deep needs 10 × 8 × 0.1 = 8m³ of topsoil.

Soil Requirements for Vegetable Gardens and Planting Beds

Vegetables have deep roots and need loose, nutrient rich soil. Aim for 250 to 300mm in a vegetable bed. A bed that is 4m × 2m at 300mm needs 4 × 2 × 0.3 = 2.4m³. Ornamental planting beds can be a little shallower at 150 to 250mm, depending on the plants.

Calculating Soil for Landscaping and Ground Levelling

When you are filling low spots or building up a sloping area, work out the average depth of fill needed across the area. Measure the depth at several points, add them up and divide by the number of readings. Then use that average in the calculator. Add 10 to 15% extra for compaction, as fill soil settles more than soil laid in a shallow layer.

How to Calculate Soil Volume

Soil Volume Formula: Length × Width × Depth

The formula is simple:

Volume (m³) = Length (m) × Width (m) × Depth (m)

All three measurements must be in metres. The result is in cubic metres, which is how bulk soil is sold in Australia.

Converting Millimetres and Centimetres into Metres

This is where many people slip up. To convert:

  • Millimetres to metres: divide by 1,000 (150mm = 0.15m)
  • Centimetres to metres: divide by 100 (15cm = 0.15m)

Common depths in metres: 100mm = 0.1m, 150mm = 0.15m, 200mm = 0.2m, 300mm = 0.3m.

Calculating Soil Volume for Rectangular and Irregular Areas

For rectangles, multiply length by width. For irregular areas, break the shape into parts:

  • Triangles: ½ × base × height
  • Circles: π × radius × radius (about 3.14 × r²)

Calculate the area of each part, multiply by depth, and add the volumes together.

Practical Soil Calculation Example

Say you are filling a garden bed that is 5 metres long and 2 metres wide, with 250mm of soil.

  • Convert the depth: 250mm = 0.25m
  • Calculate the volume: 5 × 2 × 0.25 = 2.5m³
  • Add 10% for settling: 2.5 × 1.1 = 2.75m³
  • Estimate the weight using the default topsoil density of 1.3 tonnes per m³: 2.75m³ × 1.3 = about 3.58 tonnes

The calculator’s suggested bulk order is rounded up to the next 0.5m³ step, so 2.75m³ is shown as 3.0m³. Bag counts use the calculated 2.75m³ quantity instead of that rounded bulk-order figure.

Choosing the Right Soil for Your Project

Topsoil for Lawns and General Landscaping

Topsoil is the general purpose option. It is good for levelling, building up lawn areas and filling garden borders. It usually contains a mix of sand, silt, clay and some organic matter. It is a budget friendly choice for large areas, but it may need compost mixed in for planting beds.

Garden Soil for Planting Beds

Garden soil is a more refined product, typically blended with composted organic matter. It drains well, holds moisture and gives plants a good start. It is a good choice for flower beds, shrubs and general planting.

Vegetable Garden Soil and Growing Mix

Vegetable mixes are richer in compost and organic matter, with good drainage and a loose structure. They suit raised beds and edible gardens where the soil is worked heavily each season. Look for products described as suitable for growing vegetables and herbs.

Premium Blended Soil and Specialised Soil Mixes

Premium blends combine soil, compost and other additives for specific uses. There are mixes for natives, for pots and planters, for roses and for lawns. They cost more per cubic metre, so use them where the plants really benefit, such as raised beds and feature gardens.

What Depth of Soil Do You Need?

Recommended Soil Depth for Garden Beds

Most garden beds work well with 200 to 300mm of soil. Shallow rooted plants and annuals can manage with 150 to 200mm, while shrubs and larger perennials prefer more.

Soil Depth for Growing Lawns and Turf

A layer of 100 to 150mm of topsoil gives lawn roots room to grow and hold moisture through summer. Shallower layers of 75mm can work, but lawns on thin soil tend to dry out and struggle in hot weather.

Soil Depth for Vegetable Gardens

Use 250 to 300mm for most vegetables. Root crops such as carrots and parsnips benefit from the upper end of that range, or even 350 to 400mm in a raised bed.

Factors That Affect the Required Soil Depth

The right depth depends on what you are growing, the quality of the soil underneath and how well it drains. If your existing soil is hard clay or heavily compacted, a deeper layer of new soil, or loosening the base first, helps roots establish.

Understanding Soil Volume, Weight and Delivery

How Much Does a Cubic Metre of Soil Weigh?

A cubic metre of soil usually weighs between 1.2 and 1.6 tonnes. Dry, sandy mixes are lighter. Wet or clay heavy soils can be heavier. For a quick topsoil estimate, the calculator starts at 1.3 tonnes per cubic metre. Its other soil presets use different starting densities, and the density field is editable.

Why Soil Weight Changes with Moisture Content

Soil absorbs water like a sponge. After heavy rain, the same cubic metre can weigh noticeably more than it did when dry. This matters for trailer loads and delivery limits, so if you are collecting soil yourself, check the load weight and not just the volume.

How Many Soil Bags Make Up One Cubic Metre?

The calculator lets you choose 25L, 30L, 40L or 50L bags, or enter a custom bag size. One cubic metre is 1,000 litres, so:

  • 25L bags: about 40 bags
  • 30L bags: about 33 bags
  • 40L bags: about 25 bags
  • 50L bags: about 20 bags

Check the volume printed on the bag, as sizes vary between brands.

Bulk Soil Delivery vs Bagged Soil

Bags suit small jobs, pots and top ups. They are easy to carry and store, but the cost per cubic metre is much higher. For anything over half a cubic metre, bulk delivery is usually better value. Make sure the truck can reach your property, and choose a spot where the soil can be dropped without blocking your driveway or damaging paving.

Common Soil Calculation Mistakes to Avoid

Forgetting to Convert Soil Depth into Metres

The most common error is typing 200 instead of 0.2. This makes the result 1,000 times too big. Check that your depth is in metres before you calculate, or use the calculator’s built in conversion.

Measuring Garden Beds Incorrectly

Measure the inside dimensions of raised beds, not the outside. Frames take up space, and using the outer dimensions can lead you to order too much soil. For curved beds, take several width measurements and use the average.

Not Accounting for Existing Soil Levels

If you are topping up a bed that already has soil in it, only calculate the extra depth you need. Measure the current soil level and the target level, and use the difference.

Ordering Too Much or Too Little Soil

The calculator starts with a 10% settling allowance. You can select 0%, 5%, 10%, 15% or a custom percentage. Its suggested bulk order is then rounded up to 0.5m³ steps; bag counts are based on the calculated litre volume, not that rounded bulk-order figure.

Frequently Asked Questions About the Soil Calculator

How Much Soil Do I Need for 1 Square Metre?+

It depends on the depth. At 100mm deep, 1m² needs 0.1m³. Using the calculator’s default topsoil density of 1.3t/m³, that is about 130kg. At 300mm deep, it needs 0.3m³, or about 390kg. Other soil types and moisture levels change the weight.

How Many Cubic Metres of Soil Do I Need?+

Multiply the length by the width by the depth, with all measurements in metres. For example, a bed that is 3m × 2m at 200mm deep needs 3 × 2 × 0.2 = 1.2m³. Add the calculator’s starting 10% settling allowance to get 1.32m³. Its suggested bulk order rounds that up to 1.5m³; bag counts are based on 1.32m³.

How Deep Should Soil Be for a Garden Bed?+

Aim for 200 to 300mm for most garden beds. Vegetable gardens and deep rooted plants do best at 300mm or more, while shallow beds for annuals can be 150 to 200mm.

How Much Does 1m³ of Soil Weigh?+

One cubic metre of soil typically weighs 1.2 to 1.6 tonnes. Moisture content and soil type change the figure, so ask your supplier for the exact weight of the product you choose.

How Many Bags of Soil Are in a Cubic Metre?+

It depends on the bag size. There are about 40 bags of 25L soil, 34 bags of 30L soil, 25 bags of 40L soil or 20 bags of 50L soil in a cubic metre. The calculator also accepts a custom bag volume.

Can I Calculate Soil for Multiple Garden Beds?+

Yes. Calculate the volume for each bed separately, then add the results together. This gives you a total to order in one delivery and avoids paying multiple delivery fees.
