How to Use the Paint Calculator

Enter the Dimensions of Your Walls

Measure the length of each wall and the height from floor to ceiling in metres. Most Australian homes have ceilings around 2.4 metres high, but older homes and some new builds can be taller. Measure rather than assume, as a few extra centimetres across many walls adds up.

Calculate the Total Surface Area to Be Painted

For each wall, multiply length by height. Add all the walls together to get the total wall area. The calculator does this for you once you enter the measurements. For a quick estimate of a whole room, add up the length of all four walls (the perimeter) and multiply by the ceiling height.

Include Doors, Windows and Other Openings

Doors and windows do not need paint, so subtract their area from the total. A standard door is about 2.0m × 0.8m, which is around 1.6m². A typical window might be 1.2m × 1.2m, or about 1.4m². Small openings under 1m² can usually be ignored, as the extra paint will cover cutting in and touch ups.

Select the Number of Paint Coats

Most paint jobs need two coats. A single coat is only enough when you are repainting in the same or a very similar colour over a clean, sound surface. Going from dark to light, or painting new plaster, may need three coats or a primer first.

Calculate the Total Amount of Paint Required

The calculator multiplies the painted area by the number of coats, then divides by the coverage per litre. The result is the total litres needed. Round up to the nearest tin size so you do not run short in the middle of a wall.

How Much Paint Do You Need for Your Project?

How Much Paint Is Needed for One Room?

For a room that is 3m × 3m with a 2.4m ceiling, the walls come to 12 × 2.4 = 28.8m². Subtracting about 3m² for a door and a window leaves around 25.8m². With two coats, that is about 51.6m² of paint coverage. At 14m² per litre, you need about 3.7 litres, so a 4 litre tin is the right size.

Calculating Paint for Walls and Ceilings

Ceilings are calculated separately from walls, because they usually use a different paint, often a flat or ceiling white. A 3m × 3m ceiling is 9m². For two coats, that is 18m², or roughly 1.3 litres at 14m² per litre. A 2 litre tin would be enough, with some left over.

Estimating Paint for an Entire House

For a whole house, the quickest approach is to calculate room by room and add the totals. As a rough guide, interior wall area is often around 2.5 times the floor area. A 100m² home might have about 225m² of wall area after subtracting openings. Two coats at 14m² per litre comes to around 32 litres for walls, plus around 14 litres for the ceilings.

Calculating Paint for Exterior Walls and Surfaces

Exterior walls need measuring in the same way, but they take more paint because of rougher surfaces such as render, weatherboard and brick. Measure the width and height of each wall, subtract windows and doors, and use the coverage rate recommended for exterior paint. Add 10 to 15% for textured surfaces.

How to Calculate Paint Coverage

Paint Coverage per Litre Explained

Coverage is how many square metres one litre of paint will cover with a single coat. For most interior and exterior acrylic paints, this is around 12 to 16m² per litre on a smooth, sealed surface. The exact figure is printed on the tin. Rough, porous or unpainted surfaces soak up paint and reduce the coverage.

Paint Calculation Formula: Surface Area ÷ Coverage

The formula is:

Litres = (Surface area (m²) × Number of coats) ÷ Coverage per litre (m²/L)

For example, 40m² of wall with two coats at 14m² per litre is (40 × 2) ÷ 14 = 5.7 litres.

How Multiple Paint Coats Affect the Total Quantity

Every extra coat adds the full area again. Two coats need twice the paint of one coat, and three coats need three times as much. This is why it is worth getting the coat count right before you buy.

Worked Example of a Paint Quantity Calculation

Let’s say you are painting a living room with walls that are 5m and 4m, and a ceiling height of 2.4m.

  • Calculate the perimeter: (5 + 4) × 2 = 18m
  • Calculate the wall area: 18 × 2.4 = 43.2m²
  • Subtract openings: one door (1.6m²) and two windows (2.8m²) = 4.4m². Remaining area: 38.8m²
  • Multiply by two coats: 38.8 × 2 = 77.6m²
  • Divide by coverage of 14m² per litre: 77.6 ÷ 14 = 5.5 litres

You would buy a 10 litre tin, or a 4 litre and a 2 litre tin if you want to reduce waste.

What Factors Affect How Much Paint You Need?

Wall Surface Texture and Porosity

Smooth, sealed walls give the best coverage. Rough, textured or porous surfaces such as raw plaster, brick and render soak up more paint. For these, expect coverage to drop to 8 to 10m² per litre or lower.

Existing Paint Colour and New Paint Colour

Painting a light colour over a similar light colour usually needs two coats. Going from dark to light, or covering strong colours like red or navy, may need an extra coat or a tinted primer. Going from light to dark is usually easier, but deep colours can still take three coats.

Paint Type and Product Coverage

Premium paints often have better coverage and hiding power than budget options. A cheaper paint that needs three coats may end up costing more than a better product that covers in two. Always check the coverage rate on the tin.

Primers, Undercoats and Additional Coats

Primers seal the surface and help the topcoat stick and look even. Primer coverage is typically 10 to 14m² per litre, a little lower than topcoat. Include primer in your calculation if you are painting bare surfaces, stains, patched areas or making a major colour change.

Painting New Surfaces vs Previously Painted Walls

New plasterboard, bare timber and fresh render are thirsty surfaces. They need a primer or sealer coat, followed by two topcoats. Previously painted walls in good condition are far easier and often need just two coats of topcoat.

How Much Paint Do You Need for Different Surfaces?

Paint Requirements for Interior Walls

Interior walls are the starting point for most calculations. Use a low sheen or matt acrylic for bedrooms and living areas. Allow two coats, and add a small amount for cutting in around edges, corners and trim.

Paint Requirements for Ceilings

Ceilings absorb a lot of paint because they are flat and often porous. A ceiling white typically has a slightly lower coverage rate of 12 to 14m² per litre. Calculate the ceiling area as length × width, and plan on two coats.

Exterior Wall Paint Requirements

Exterior walls face sun, rain and temperature swings, so use a product made for outdoor use. Rendered and brick surfaces absorb more paint than painted weatherboard. Plan for 10 to 15% extra on rough surfaces and use a suitable primer on bare or chalky areas.

Paint Requirements for Doors, Windows and Trim

Doors, window frames, skirting boards and architraves are usually painted in a semi gloss or gloss. A standard door painted on both sides covers about 3.2m², so two coats need roughly 6.4m² of coverage, or about 0.5 litres. For trim, a 1 litre tin is usually enough for a single room.

Choosing the Right Paint for Your Project

Interior Paint vs Exterior Paint

Interior paints are designed for low odour, easy cleaning and a smooth finish. Exterior paints contain additives that resist UV, mould and moisture. Do not use interior paint outside, as it will fade and break down quickly in Australian sun.

Water-Based vs Oil-Based Paint

Water based (acrylic) paints are the most common choice. They dry quickly, have low odour and clean up with water. Oil based or solvent based paints give a very hard, durable finish, which suits trim and doors, but they take longer to dry, smell stronger and need mineral turpentine for cleanup.

Choosing Paint Finishes for Different Rooms

  • Flat or matt: ceilings and low traffic rooms, hides imperfections
  • Low sheen: living rooms and bedrooms, easy to wipe
  • Semi gloss: kitchens, bathrooms, doors and trim, resists moisture and marks
  • Gloss: accents, doors and joinery, very hard wearing

When Do You Need a Primer or Undercoat?

Use a primer on bare plaster, bare timber, patched areas, stained surfaces and when changing colour dramatically. Primer improves adhesion, blocks stains and helps the topcoat look even. If you are repainting sound, clean paint in a similar colour, you can usually skip it.

Tips for Buying the Right Amount of Paint

Understanding Paint Tin Sizes and Coverage

Paint in Australia is commonly sold in 1, 2, 4, 10 and 15 litre tins, with some brands also offering 20 litres. At 14m² per litre, a 4 litre tin covers about 56m² for one coat and 28m² for two coats. Compare the cost per litre, as the larger tins are usually cheaper per litre.

How to Reduce Paint Waste

Measure carefully, round up only to the next tin size, and buy all your paint for one colour at the same time. Pour a little into a smaller container for cutting in, which keeps the main tin clean. Close lids firmly after each session.

Why You Should Keep Some Extra Paint for Touch-Ups

Walls pick up scuffs, marks and dents over time. Keeping a small amount of leftover paint, around 500ml to 1 litre, means you can fix marks quickly and match the original colour. Different batches can vary slightly, so having the original is safer than trying to match later.

How to Store Leftover Paint Properly

Wipe the rim, press the lid on firmly and store the tin in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight and extreme heat. Label the tin with the colour name, brand, room and date. In hot parts of Australia, avoid sheds and garages that get very hot, as heat can spoil water based paint.

Frequently Asked Questions About the Paint Calculator

How Much Paint Do I Need for a 3m × 3m Room?+

With a 2.4m ceiling, the walls are about 28.8m² before subtracting a door and window. After deducting about 3m², you have around 26m². For two coats at 14m² per litre, you need about 3.7 litres of wall paint, so one 4 litre tin. The ceiling needs roughly 1.3 litres more.

How Many Square Metres Does 1 Litre of Paint Cover?+

Most paints cover around 12 to 16m² per litre for a single coat on a smooth, sealed wall. Rough or porous surfaces reduce this to 8 to 10m² per litre. The tin will list the exact coverage for that product.

How Many Coats of Paint Should I Apply?+

Two coats is the standard for a durable, even finish. You may only need one if you are repainting the same colour over a clean, sound surface. New plaster, dramatic colour changes or poor coverage paint may need three coats.

Should I Subtract Windows and Doors When Calculating Paint?+

Yes, for large openings. Subtract about 1.6m² for a standard door and the actual area for each window. For small openings under 1m², you can usually ignore them, as the extra paint will cover cutting in and touch ups.

How Much Paint Do I Need for a 100m² House?+

For a 100m² floor area, wall area is often around 225m² after subtracting openings. Two coats at 14m² per litre comes to roughly 32 litres for walls and 14 litres for ceilings. This is an estimate, so measure your own home for a better result.

Does Wall Texture Affect Paint Coverage?+

Yes. Rough and textured walls have more surface area and soak up more paint than smooth walls. Coverage can drop to 8 to 10m² per litre, so add 10 to 15% to your total for textured surfaces.
